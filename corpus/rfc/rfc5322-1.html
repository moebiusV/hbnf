RFC 5322: addr-spec

rfc5322-1.bnf is addr-spec (section 3.4.1) and every rule it reaches, 27 rules, verbatim, in the order the RFC defines them, including the obsolete syntax (obs-FWS, obs-local-part, obs-domain, ...). rfc5322-1.hbnf is the same text with the one line dialect abnf. <id>.vectors/ holds inputs for ten of them; addr-spec (29 inputs, with the obsolete syntax) runs both files, in C, and the .hbnf in all four backends.

What hbnf says to the RFC's text

It compiles as written under --abnf. Three things happen on the way, and each is said:

  • "\" is a backslash. In HyperBNF's own notation a backslash in a string starts an escape and "\" never closes; in ABNF it is one character. Under --abnf, and with the line dialect abnf, strings have no escapes. (Without it, hbnf says: "this string literal does not end on its line. A backslash in "..." starts an escape ... write a backslash as \\ or %x5C".)
  • atom and word are renamed. In RFC 5322 they are rules that build structures; in HyperBNF atom and word name built-in types that read as one value, in every backend. The reader calls the rules atom-rule and word-rule, in the tree as well, wherever the grammar refers to them, and says so.
  • A range in a rule that names other rules (`dtext = %d33-90 / %d94-126 / obs-dtext) is a rule of its own, which the reader writes (dtext-x21-5A`, dtext-x5E-7E) and refers to.

The obsolete forms are supported. Their alternatives overlap by design: the obs- rules accept what the current ones do, and more (obs-local-part takes every dot-atom and every quoted-string, with white space around the dots), and RFC 5322 says to accept them and never to generate them. A union that needs backtracking (word = atom / quoted-string, local-part, domain, FWS) is taken as first match wins, as RFC 3986 section 3.2.2 settles host: the first alternative that matches and is followed by something that can follow the choice. hbnf warns once for each such union and names the two alternatives, and the vectors check that the result accepts what the RFC's union accepts.

What the vectors say about the text itself

addr-spec accepts john@example.com, a quoted local part, a domain literal, and the obsolete forms (a . b@example.com, comments and folding white space around the parts); it refuses what the RFC refuses.

Speed

Generating the whole of this text takes about nine seconds, most of it deciding the unions above. RFC 3986's takes two.
